Making IamExpat the first answer for expats in Germany

IamExpat already has the knowledge newcomers need. This case study looks at how to make that knowledge easier to read on a phone, and easier for Google and AI tools to find and recommend.

in short

  • The gap: IamExpat ranks well on Google but is almost invisible in AI answers, and its long guides are hard to scan on a phone.
  • What I did: an AI citation test, a page review against competitors, a research review, and a working prototype.
  • The idea: keep all of IamExpat's content, but lead with the answer and lay it out as tap-to-open steps.

why this case study exists

The goal: when an expat in Germany has a question, IamExpat is where they find the answer.

Expats now look for answers in two places: Google, and AI tools like ChatGPT. This case study works on both:

  • Visibility: help IamExpat's guides rank in Google and get cited by AI tools.
  • Reading experience: help a reader on a phone find their answer and their next step in seconds.

Two ideas I set out to test

Idea 1 · AI visibility

If guides lead with a clear answer, back it with sources and lay out the steps, AI tools will cite IamExpat more often.

Idea 2 · Reader experience

If guides are organised around the reader's situation and next step, phone readers will find what they need faster and stay longer.

Checked by Test 2, the page review, and supported by existing research.

How easy are the pages to use on a phone?

How I scored the pages

Does it give me the answer straight away? Does it tell me what to do first? Are the steps in order? Can I find my situation? Is it easy to skim? Are there practical details? Are sources and dates shown? Does it tell me what to do next? Each answer scored 0, 1 or 2.

FindingWhat it looks like
The facts are strongEvery IamExpat guide scored full points for documents, fees, deadlines and German terms.
The start and end are weakPages open with general context instead of the answer, and end without a next step.
Order winsAll About Berlin has similar facts but numbered steps, and scores 14 vs 10. On IamExpat, the first steps start two-thirds down the page.
Topic pages have no starting pointThe immigration page lists immigration lawyers before the visa guide, and Anmeldung after permanent residence.
Headings could helpCompetitors use questions ("Can my boss fire me during my pregnancy?"). IamExpat uses labels ("Choosing a hospital"). A pattern, not yet proven.

existing research

04

What we already know about reading on phones

StatCounter

Most reading happens on phones

60% of web traffic in Germany was mobile in September 2026, 38% desktop.

Nielsen Norman Group

People skim

Readers have time for at most 28% of the words on a page, and about 20% is more realistic.

GOV.UK

Step-by-step works for journeys

The UK government built its step-by-step pattern for multi-task journeys after 8 rounds of user research.

Nielsen Norman Group

Toggles suit phones

Expandable sections save space and act as a mini table of contents, if built carefully.

Google · Vercel

Toggles stay readable to Google and AI

As long as the text sits in the page itself rather than loading later.

Aggarwal et al. 2024

Sources help AI pick a page

Adding cited sources and statistics made pages up to 40% more visible in AI answers.

The evidence supports step-by-step layouts for task-based guides. For pages people read start to finish, a clear summary at the top may be enough.

05

The problem

Expats arrive at stressful moments, usually on a phone. The right information is there, but the answer and next step are buried, and AI tools pick clearer competitor guides instead.

How might we help a newcomer on their phone find their next step on IamExpat in seconds, in a way AI tools can also pick up?

solutions

Eight options, one focused pilot

The pilot uses the low-effort options that help readers and AI with the same change.

measuring success

08

A small pilot IamExpat can measure with its own data

No recruiting needed: rebuild a few guides and compare them with their own "before" numbers.

  1. Pick 3 guidesStep-based topics such as pregnancy, residence permit and Anmeldung
  2. Record the baselineCurrent analytics, plus the AI citation test
  3. Rebuild and publishApply options A, B, D, E and F
  4. Measure for 6–8 weeksCompare with the baseline
  5. Re-run the AI testAfter 1–2 months, same questions
